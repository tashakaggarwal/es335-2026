The Question & Unit of Analysis

Research Question
Given the state of an IPL run chase after each completed over, how much probabilistic accuracy (measured by log loss and calibration) does a learned model add over the broadcast heuristic “win if current run rate ≥ required run rate”, and does that edge survive the 2024–2026 scoring inflation?
What One Row Represents
One row is the exact snapshot of a regular second-innings chase at the end of completed over o ∈ {1, …, 19} (before the 20th over starts or the chase finishes earlier).
Target Variable & Output
chasing_team_won ∈ {0, 1} (1 if the chasing team wins the match, 0 otherwise). The model outputs a calibrated probability p̂ ∈ (0, 1), not a binary label.
Prediction-Time Information Boundary
Predictions are made immediately after the final delivery of completed over o. Available information is strictly limited to pre-match context from dates prior to the match, the first-innings target, deliveries up to over o, and the incoming batter at the crease for over o + 1—never any future delivery or outcome.

Data Source, Size & Exclusions

Source & Volume
Cricsheet ball-by-ball JSON files for the IPL (2008–2026). Out of 1,243 raw files parsed by scripts/parse_cricsheet.py, we keep 1,185 completed regular chases yielding 21,251 second-innings over states (plus 22,438 first-innings states used strictly on training seasons to fit the empirical DLS curve).
58 Excluded Matches (Logged)
Every excluded file is recorded with a reason in data/parsed/match_log.csv: 25 ties/super-overs or no-results (tie_or_no_result), 23 rain-interrupted DLS matches (dls_applied), and 10 shortened matches (reduced_overs).
Quality Issues Resolved
(1) Era shift: mean targets jumped from 165.8 (train) to 195.4 (test). (2) Entity drift: 60 raw stadium spellings were unified into 36 canonical grounds, and renamed franchises (e.g., Delhi Daredevils → Delhi Capitals, Kings XI Punjab → Punjab Kings) were mapped. (3) Ball accounting: wides/no-balls add runs without consuming legal balls; retired hurt is not a wicket; crease batters at over-end are read from the first ball of the next over (showing who is present, never future outcomes).

Preprocessing Choices & Why

1. Era-Adjusted Target (target_vs_venue_par)
Raw target fails under scoring inflation (200 in 2012 was near-impossible; in 2025 it is par). Subtracting the past-only venue average target (shrunk toward the 30-match rolling league par) reduces out-of-range test rows to 0.8%.
2. Empirical Exponential DLS Curve
Linear balls_left × wickets_in_hand treats all overs and wickets as interchangeable. We fit a Duckworth–Lewis–Stern curve Z(u, w) = Z0(w)[1 − e−b(w)u] strictly on 2008–2023 first innings (dls_params.json) to compute non-linear dls_resources and par_score_diff.
3. Capping required_run_rate at 36.0
36 runs/over is six sixes off six legal balls. Uncapped late ratios (e.g., 30 needed off 1 ball → RRR = 180) create extreme leverage points that distort linear weights.
4. Empirical Bayes Shrinkage & Recency Weights
Debutant/small-sample player strike rates (shrunk toward 125 over 60 pseudo-balls), balls/dismissal (toward 18), venue chase rates (toward league mean), and 5-match form (toward 0.5) prevent noisy extremes. Exponential season weights w = e−0.08(max_season − season) prioritize modern T20 dynamics.

Chronological Split by Season (Zero Match Leakage)

Splitting randomly by row would put Over 10 of a match in Train and Over 11 of the same match in Test, leaking the match trajectory. We split strictly by calendar season (scripts/data_split.py) and keep all overs of a match in the same fold:

5. Baselines, Candidate Models & Validation Selection (Notebook 03 ↗)

Models Compared, Evaluation Protocol & Feature Selection

Before touching the 2025–2026 test seasons, three baselines and four machine learning architectures were tuned via 5-fold GroupKFold on 2008–2023 and compared on the 2024 validation season (70 matches, 1,239 over states).

Evaluation Metrics & Uncertainty Protocol

Primary Metric: Log Loss (Cross-Entropy)
Because our goal is a calibrated probability rather than a binary decision, log loss is the primary proper scoring rule: it heavily penalizes confident wrong predictions (e.g., predicting 98% when a team collapses). Probabilities are clipped to [10−6, 1 − 10−6].
Secondary Metrics: Brier Score & Accuracy
Brier score measures mean squared probability error mean((p̂ − y)2). Accuracy (p̂ ≥ 0.5) is reported for interpretability, though late overs are mostly decided and flatter all models.
Match-Cluster Bootstrap (2,000 Replicates)
The 19 overs of a single match are strongly correlated; treating rows as independent dramatically understates uncertainty. All 95% confidence intervals and paired differences vs. Baseline B resample whole matches (N_BOOT = 2000).
Pre-Registered Parsimony Selection Rule
Comparing ~18 model and feature-stage candidates on a single 70-match validation season invites selection optimism (winner’s curse). We fixed our selection rule in advance: pick the simplest candidate (fewest features, linear before trees) within 0.003 validation log loss of the overall best.

Data Leakage Controls & Reproducibility

Chronological Splits & Match Grouping
Train (2008–2023), Validation (2024), and Test (2025–2026) are separated strictly by calendar year. Hyperparameter tuning uses 5-fold GroupKFold(groups=match_id); gradient boosting runs with early_stopping=False because built-in early stopping splits randomly by row.
Brute-Force Verified Past-Only Features
All historical features (batter SR/BPD, bowler economy, venue par/chase rate, home edge, 5-match form) exclude same-day and future matches—verified against a brute-force recomputation across 60 sampled matches in Notebook 02 with 0 disagreements.
In-Fold Preprocessing & Out-of-Fold Calibration
SimpleImputer(strategy="median") and StandardScaler() live inside scikit-learn Pipeline objects so statistics are refitted inside every CV fold. Platt and isotonic recalibrators are fit strictly on out-of-fold training predictions.
Single-Shot Locked Test & Profiling Honesty Note
Notebooks 01 and 03 assert season < 2025, and Notebook 04 writes a lock file (test_results.json) after one run. Honesty note: while no model tuning or selection ever touched 2025–2026 rows, aggregate season-level match counts and average target scores across the parsed Cricsheet archive (the 2024–2026 scoring surge) were seen during initial dataset profiling, motivating scale-free and par-normalized features.

Why Linear Beat Trees & Why Recalibration Was Unnecessary

Why Logistic Regression Outperformed Boosted Trees & MLP
Once domain non-linearities are explicitly encoded via the empirical DLS curve (dls_resources, par_score_diff) and ratio terms (runs_needed_per_wicket, run_rate_diff), the log-odds of winning are nearly linear in the standardized features. Axis-aligned tree splits produce step-function probability jumps and overfit match-level idiosyncrasies across ~977 training matches.
Out-of-Fold Recalibration Check (Notebook 03 §10)
We tested Platt scaling and isotonic regression fitted on 5-fold out-of-fold training predictions:
  • LR raw: 0.3690 log loss (0.1174 Brier)
  • LR + Platt scaling: 0.3695 log loss (0.1175 Brier)
  • LR + Isotonic regression: 0.3707 log loss (0.1185 Brier)
  • HGB raw vs Platt vs Isotonic: 0.3899 vs 0.3898 vs 0.4085
Because L2 logistic regression directly optimizes log loss, post-hoc recalibration offered no gain and was omitted from the final pipeline.

7. Conclusion, Limitations, Reproduction Steps & Disclosure

Conclusion, Scope & How to Reproduce

Conclusion: Answering the Research Question

Yes—a parsimonious, domain-informed linear model substantially and reliably outperforms the broadcast run-rate heuristic on future IPL seasons while remaining well calibrated under severe scoring inflation:

  1. Large, Statistically Decisive Gain Over the Run-Rate Rule: On 138 unseen 2025–2026 test matches (2,434 over states), our pre-selected 11-feature L2 logistic regression (B +derived) achieved a log loss of 0.3815 (Brier 0.1219, accuracy 82.05%) versus 0.5396 log loss (Brier 0.1792, accuracy 75.31%) for the textbook run-rate rule (CRR ≥ RRR). The paired improvement of −0.1576 log loss (95% match-bootstrap CI [−0.2126, −0.0985], P(better) = 100%) beat the heuristic in 104 of 138 matches (75.4%).
  2. Domain Feature Engineering Beat Model Complexity: Once non-linear resource depletion (dls_resources, par_score_diff fitted on 2008–2023 first innings) and wicket-adjusted rate ratios (runs_needed_per_wicket) were explicitly encoded, L2 logistic regression outperformed gradient-boosted trees (0.3899), monotonic boosted trees (0.3901), and a neural network (0.3856) on validation, and needed no post-hoc Platt or isotonic recalibration.
  3. Robustness to Era Shift & What Remains Unpredictable: Normalizing targets against past-only venue par (target_vs_venue_par) reduced out-of-range test rows from 37.7% to 0.8%, allowing the model to achieve its strongest performance in 2026 (0.3428 log loss, 84.09% accuracy). Remaining errors are concentrated in genuinely open middle-over coin flips (RRR ∈ (8, 12]) and rare T20 tail-event reversals (sudden 8-wicket collapses or lower-order miracles).

    Step-by-Step Reproduction & Repository Guide

    All 1,243 raw Cricsheet JSON files, scripts, notebooks, and split CSVs are committed in github.com/tashakaggarwal/es335-2026 (README.md). Reproduce from scratch in Python 3.10+ (RANDOM_STATE = 0):

    1. Install dependencies (requirements.txt):
      pip install -r requirements.txt
    2. Parse raw Cricsheet JSONs (parse_cricsheet.py):
      python scripts/parse_cricsheet.py data/raw --out data/parsed/overs.csv
      Writes overs.csv (21,251 rows), first_innings_states.csv (22,438 rows), and match_log.csv (1,243 files).

      Fits dls_params.json on season < 2024, verifies 0 disagreements on past-only history features, and writes data/features/features_all.csv.
    4. Create chronological season splits (data_split.py):
      python scripts/data_split.py → writes data/split/{train,validation,test}.csv.
    5. Run EDA & model selection (01_eda, 03_baselines_and_models):
      Tunes all models via 5-fold GroupKFold, runs the 7-stage feature ladder, and freezes results/model_selection.json.
    6. Run locked test evaluation & export site data (04_final_test, 05_export_site_data):
      Scores the 2025–2026 test set once (locks results/final/test_results.json), exports site/data/site_data.json, and serves locally via python -m http.server 8000.
